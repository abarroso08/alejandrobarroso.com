451 is the temperature at which books burn. This book describes a dystopian future where houses use non-flammable paint and firemen burn books instead of extinguishing fires. 

The main idea I take from the book is that society tends to follow the path of least resistance. The consumption of easy content and the avoidance of challenging ideas undermines critical thinking in society. Books are a door to criticism, confusion and debate, so reading is forbidden. This leads to a passive population with no deeper purpose.

The main character, Montag, is a fireman who experiences a series of internal contradictions that make him search for answers. The primary catalyst for his change is Clarisse, a young girl who ignores societal rules simply to experience true joy.

Ultimately, Fahrenheit 451 served as a powerful reminder of how easy it is to trade meaningful thought for instant comfort. What struck me the most was how relevant Bradbury's vision remains today. We don't need government to ban books if we voluntarily choose to stop thinking critically. I recommend this book to anyone who wants to reflect on our modern relationship with technology, media, and quick distraction.
